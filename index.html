What we build

Machines and materials that put waste to work

Flue gas, dirty water, river plastic and farm waste are raw materials to us. Everything here is designed, built and installed in Nepal.

Flagship

Vayu Putra Algae Reactor

An air-pollution device that feeds carbon dioxide, sunlight and wastewater to algae, which give back oxygen and biomass. Built for hospitals, waste incinerators, factories and municipalities that release flue gas.

Rivers

Bagmati River Trash Barrier

Floating barriers that stop plastic and other waste before it travels down the Bagmati. Built under an MoU with the High Powered Commission, and a finalist at Falling Walls Lab Kathmandu 2024.

Local governments

Carbon Baseline Registration

84 municipalities across Nepal have written to Chyau Bio Technologies to take part. Organizations in those municipalities register their climate and business activity to set a documented carbon baseline, run on the Carbon Updates platform.

Farming

Smart Farming Tools

The Smart Switch sensor kit sends sunlight, temperature, soil moisture and humidity readings to the farmer's phone. Our Smart Farming PPE protects farmers while they spray crops, and we run greenhouse training for growers.

Structures

Geodesic Domes

Light, strong dome frames for greenhouses and growing spaces, assembled from our own numbered construction manual in English and Nepali.

Where we started

Mycelium Biomaterials

Our first research project: mycelium grown on farm waste to make biodegradable insulation panels. It is also where our name comes from: chyau means mushroom in Nepali.

How the algae reactor works

Polluted air in, oxygen out

Algae photosynthesise far faster than trees. The reactor gives them the carbon dioxide from a chimney, nutrients from wastewater and the sunlight around them.

☀ Sunlight+Algae+Wastewater+CO2→Oxygen+Biomass

Hospitals

Medical waste incinerators must run by law. A reactor on site turns their flue gas into oxygen.

Waste incinerators

Treat the smoke from waste burning instead of releasing it.

Factories and kilns

Cement plants, kilns and boilers produce CO2 in bulk: feedstock for algae.

Municipalities

Wastewater treatment, cleaner public spaces and algal biomass for energy.

ICIMOD reports our first two reactors, at Pulchowk and Jawalakhel, produced more than 12,000 litres of oxygen, from a machine with a footprint under 35 sq ft. Scale-up tests continue at Impact Hub FabLab, with a reactor connected to a plastic recycling facility. More videos →

Our story

From mushrooms to machines

Chyau means mushroom in Nepali, and that is where we started. In 2019 a team of engineers, research scientists, activists and economists began working on sustainable technology in Kathmandu. Our first project grew mycelium on agricultural waste as a low-cost, biodegradable alternative to home insulation.

Since then our main work has been the Vayu Putra algae reactor. We installed a 1,000-litre reactor at Pulchowk on World Environment Day 2022, and we now also build trash barriers for the Bagmati River and smart tools for farmers.

We work on unit economics as hard as on the science: we design, build and install in Nepal so that the people who need these machines can afford them.

  1. 2019

    Presented the algae reactor research to Shakti Bahadur Basnet, Minister of Energy, Water Resources and Irrigation.

  5. 5 June 2024

    Won the Green Startup Achievement Award at the Waste Management & Climate Conference 2024.

  6. 20 July 2024

    Finalist at Falling Walls Lab Kathmandu, organised by DAAD and Kathmandu University, with the Bagmati River trash barrier.

  7. Today

    84 municipalities across Nepal working with us on carbon baseline registration, run on the Carbon Updates platform.
