What we build

Machines and materials that put waste to work

Flue gas, dirty water, river plastic and farm waste are raw materials to us. Everything here is designed, built and installed in Nepal.

Flagship

Vayu Putra Algae Reactor

An air-pollution device that feeds carbon dioxide, sunlight and wastewater to algae, which give back oxygen and biomass. Built for hospitals, waste incinerators, factories and municipalities that release flue gas.

Rivers

Bagmati River Trash Barrier

Floating barriers that stop plastic and other waste before it travels down the Bagmati. Built under an MoU with the High Powered Commission, and a finalist at Falling Walls Lab Kathmandu 2024.

Local governments

Carbon Baseline Registration

84 municipalities across Nepal have written to Chyau Bio Technologies to take part. Organizations in those municipalities register their climate and business activity to set a documented carbon baseline, run on the Carbon Updates platform.

Farming

Smart Farming Tools

The Smart Switch sensor kit sends sunlight, temperature, soil moisture and humidity readings to the farmer's phone. Our Smart Farming PPE protects farmers while they spray crops, and we run greenhouse training for growers.

Structures

Geodesic Domes

Light, strong dome frames for greenhouses and growing spaces, assembled from our own numbered construction manual in English and Nepali. How the dome works →

Where we started

Mycelium Biomaterials

Our first research project: mycelium grown on farm waste to make biodegradable insulation panels. It is also where our name comes from: chyau means mushroom in Nepali.

How the algae reactor works

Polluted air in, oxygen out

Algae photosynthesise far faster than trees. The Vayu Putra reactor feeds them carbon dioxide from a chimney, nutrients from wastewater and the light around them, and collects what they give back.

Geodesic domes

A greenhouse anyone can put up

A geodesic dome spreads its load across a web of triangles, so a light frame stands strong against wind and snow and encloses the most space for the least material.

Our rectangular Geodome comes as a kit of colour-coded struts and numbered joints. The construction manual, in English and Nepali, shows every beam and bolt: connect them in numbered order and the frame goes up without special tools or a trained crew. Farmers use it as a greenhouse, and it can house an algae reactor.

Our story

From mushrooms to machines

Chyau means mushroom in Nepali, and that is where we started: growing mycelium on agricultural waste as a low-cost, biodegradable alternative to home insulation.

Since then our main work has been the Vayu Putra algae reactor, alongside trash barriers for the Bagmati River, geodesic domes and shelters, and smart tools for farmers. We design, build and install in Nepal, so the people who need these machines can afford them.

  1. 2019

    Founded in Kathmandu

    A team of engineers, scientists, activists and economists starts researching mycelium insulation grown on farm waste.

  2. 2020

    ClimateLaunchpad Nepal

    Pitched our mycelium insulation panels at ClimateLaunchpad Nepal 2020.

  3. 9 October 2020

    Registered as a company

    Incorporated as Chyau Bio Technologies Pvt. Ltd. in Lalitpur.

  4. 5 June 2022

    First 1,000-litre reactor

    The Vayu Putra algae reactor goes live at Pulchowk on World Environment Day.

  5. 2022

    In the news

    Onlinekhabar English profiles our work on Kathmandu's air and waste problems; ICIMOD features our Pulchowk and Jawalakhel reactors, which have produced more than 12,000 litres of oxygen.

  6. August 2023

    Presented to the Minister

    Took the algae reactor research to Shakti Bahadur Basnet, Minister of Energy, Water Resources and Irrigation.

  7. 2024

    Bagmati trash barrier MoU

    Signed an MoU with the High Powered Commission to build trash barriers that catch floating waste in the Bagmati River.

  8. 5 June 2024

    Green Startup Achievement Award

    Won at the Waste Management & Climate Conference 2024.

  9. 20 July 2024

    Falling Walls Lab finalist

    One of ten finalists at the first Falling Walls Lab in Nepal (DAAD and Kathmandu University), with the Bagmati trash barrier.

  10. 24 June 2025

    Geodesic disaster shelter

    Finished setting up a geodesic disaster shelter, made and built by women, and began offering flat-pack shelter homes.

  11. 2025

    84 municipalities on board

    84 municipalities across Nepal write to Chyau Bio to join carbon baseline registration, run on the Carbon Updates platform.
